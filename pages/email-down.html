What to Do When Your Business Email Goes Down

By Borderland IT Solutions | February 6, 2026

Email is the backbone of business communication. When your email system goes down, it can disrupt client communications, internal workflows, and even revenue. Knowing what to do immediately—and how to prevent future outages—can save your business time, money, and headaches.

Step 1: Assess the Scope of the Outage

Determine whether the email outage affects just one user, your entire company, or external recipients. Check if other services from your provider, such as cloud storage or collaboration tools, are also affected. Understanding the scope helps you decide the urgency and the correct solution path.

Step 2: Check Your Internet and Network

Many email issues are not caused by the email provider itself. Start by checking your internet connection, router, and firewall settings. Rebooting your network hardware can sometimes restore email connectivity quickly.

Step 3: Communicate With Your Team and Clients

While troubleshooting, inform your team about the outage and temporary workarounds. If you expect the outage to last, notify clients via phone, social media, or an alternative email address. Transparency helps maintain trust and prevents frustration.

Step 4: Contact Your Email Provider

Reach out to your email hosting provider or IT partner to report the issue. They can confirm whether the problem is on their end, provide updates, or escalate to higher-level support. Having a professional IT partner ensures faster resolution and prevents downtime from dragging on.

Step 5: Implement Temporary Solutions

If the outage is prolonged, consider temporary solutions such as:

  • Forwarding emails to a backup account
  • Using web-based email access if your client software is offline
  • Communicating with clients through alternative channels

These steps minimize the impact on your operations until email is fully restored.

Step 6: Investigate Root Causes and Prevention

Once your email is back online, analyze the root cause to prevent future outages. Common causes include server misconfigurations, authentication errors, software updates, or security breaches. A proactive IT partner can implement monitoring, backup email solutions, and policies that reduce downtime risk.

Conclusion

Email outages are frustrating but manageable when you know the right steps. From assessing the problem and communicating with your team to contacting your provider and implementing temporary workarounds, proper preparation ensures minimal disruption.
